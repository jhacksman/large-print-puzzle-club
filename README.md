# Large Print Puzzle Club

A hackerspace project idea for people who like making puzzles and for activity coordinators who need something they can photocopy ten minutes before a group arrives. The first milestone is one enjoyable session run from a five-page packet. This repository is the project brief. A pack and generator are still to be built.

## Two ways to pick it up

**Make printable puzzle packs.** Build a small collection of original themed puzzles with answer keys. A first edition could be *In the Garden*. On one page, find six garden words in a grid. On another, solve clues such as “I have teeth and gather fallen leaves” (rake). Give each activity a gentler and a trickier way to play. Offer a free sampler. A possible paid edition is a month of activities for $12 with permission for one facility to print copies for its groups. Price and demand are open questions.

**Try a puzzle club at a senior center.** A hackerspace volunteer could offer one short session to an interested activity coordinator. The coordinator chooses the theme, session length and whether people work alone, in pairs, or by calling out answers. Bring spare pencils and copies. Someone who prefers listening should be able to play from the spoken clues. A center can try this idea using hand-made pages while the generator is still on the drawing board.

## First contribution

1. Pick one theme and write a five-page black-and-white packet: a word search, a clue game, a word sort, a facilitator sheet, and an answer key. Include an easy starting clue on each activity.
2. Start with 20-point clues, high contrast, generous writing space and clear grid letters. Print at actual size on the center's ordinary printer. Check the copy of a copy. Ask the coordinator what print size and format works for their group.
3. Have a second person use the key to solve the exact printed pages. A generator should repeat this check for every edition: locate each listed word, check page bounds, and generate the matching key from the final layout.
4. Offer the sampler to one willing coordinator. Ask which page they would use again, what needed explaining, and whether they would choose another free or paid pack. Record aggregate feedback and paper or layout fixes. Keep participant names and photos out of the repository.

The most useful first pull request is the printable sampler, its editable source, and one paragraph about what happened when someone printed and tried it. Anyone taking the generator route can then automate the layout that worked.
